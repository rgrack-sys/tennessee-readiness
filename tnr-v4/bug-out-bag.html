The Bug-Out BagWhat It Is & Why You Need It

If you had ten minutes to leave home, what would you grab? A bug-out bag answers that question ahead of time — packed, checked and ready by the door.

See the Gear List Get the Printable
The Basics

What Is a Bug-Out Bag?

A bug-out bag — some agencies call it a go-bag — is a pre-packed bag you can grab and carry if you have to leave in a hurry: a wildfire, a flood, a chemical spill, a house fire, or a road home that's suddenly closed. Ready.gov notes that after an emergency you may need to get by on your own for several days. Your bag is what lets you do that when you can't stay home.

It's different from a home emergency kit. A home kit can be big and heavy because it doesn't move. A bug-out bag has to go wherever your feet do.

The Principle

Every item must justify its weight. Carry what solves a realistic problem for your environment, route, health, skills and how long you expect to be out.

🎒

Main Bug-Out Bag

about 60–80 liters

The full kit for leaving home for several days: shelter, sleep system, water treatment, food, fire, first aid, tools and spare clothing.

🚗

Get-Home Bag

about 30 liters · ~35 lb

A smaller bag kept in the truck or at work, built to get you home on foot if the roads close or your vehicle quits.

👨‍👩‍👧

One per Person

sized to the carrier

Each person who can carry a bag should have their own, packed for their own needs — medications, glasses, clothing sizes.

The pack sizes above come from one of our own working kits — they're an example, not a rule. The right size is whatever you can actually carry.

Equipment Reference

What It Is & Why You Need It

💧 Water & Hydration

Ready.gov plans on one gallon per person per day — and a gallon of water weighs over 8 pounds. You can't carry days of it, so carry some and bring a way to treat more.

Titanium canteen
A durable, lightweight water container. You need a way to carry water after you collect or treat it.
Water filtration bottle
Lets you treat water from uncertain sources. Carrying water is only part of the problem; you also need a way to get more safely.
Specialized filter / cartridge
Adds filtering for specific contaminants. Choose the cartridge for the risks where you are and the filter system you actually use.

🥫 Food

Freeze-dried food
Lots of calories for very little weight, and it stores well. Remember that most freeze-dried meals need water to prepare.

🧥 Clothing

Waterproof jacket
Keeps rain from soaking your insulating layers and helps reduce heat loss in wet weather.
Waterproof pants
Protects your lower body from rain and wet brush, keeping your base layers dry.
Beanie / cold-weather hat
Lots of warmth for almost no weight or space — useful whenever the temperature drops.
Socks
Dry socks protect your feet from moisture and blisters, and keep you walking.
Underwear
A dry change helps hygiene and comfort and prevents skin problems over several days.
Spare shirt & pants
A dry set to change into if what you're wearing gets soaked, torn or contaminated.
Waterproof change-of-clothing bag
Keeps your dry clothes dry, even if the pack itself gets wet.

⛺ Shelter & Sleep

Poncho / tarp
Rain protection that can also be a quick shelter, ground cover or simple tarp.
Compact sleeping bag
Insulation and sleep protection for the temperatures you expect. Our example kit uses a bag rated around 20°F — choose the rating for your climate and season.
Emergency Mylar blanket
A very light backup for warmth, wind-blocking, signaling or emergency cover.

🔥 Fire Starting

Carry more than one way to light a fire, and keep at least one of them waterproof.

Waterproof / storm matches
A reliable way to light a fire that's designed to work better in damp conditions.
Waterproof match case
Protects matches from rain, dunking and humidity so they still work when you need them.
Mini flares / high-intensity fire starter
A stronger backup for wet or difficult conditions when ordinary tinder won't catch.
Prepared fire starters
Easy-to-light material so you aren't depending on finding dry natural tinder.
Dryer lint with petroleum jelly
Light, cheap tinder that burns longer than plain lint and helps get bigger fuel going.
Petroleum jelly
Stretches the burn time of improvised tinder and doubles as skin protection.
Hand sanitizer
Keeps hands clean when washing water is short; alcohol-based sanitizer is flammable and can also help start a fire.

🔦 Lighting

Headlamp
Hands-free light for walking, repairs, cooking, first aid and camp tasks after dark.
Small flashlight
A second, independent light and a focused beam when a headlamp isn't the right tool.
Glow sticks
Simple low light with no batteries, handy for marking a spot or a piece of gear.
Directional glow-stick cover
Lets you dim, hide or aim glow-stick light instead of lighting up everything around you.

⛑️ Medical / First Aid

Ready.gov's kit list includes a first aid kit and your prescription medications — pack enough of your own meds for the days you'd be away.

Moleskin
Treats hot spots and blisters before they get bad enough to stop you walking.
Knee brace
Supports a known weak or injured knee. Swap in whatever brace or support fits your own recurring joint problems.
Face mask
Basic protection from dust, debris, smoke and crowded or dirty conditions.
Silver-impregnated cloth / dressing
A compact wound-care item for covering and protecting minor wounds. Use supplies you understand and are trained to use.
Rag / general-purpose cloth
Cleaning, drying, padding, handling hot gear and a dozen improvised jobs.

🧼 Hygiene

Baby wipes
Basic personal cleaning when showers and running water aren't available.
Toothbrush & toothpaste
Keeps up dental hygiene and helps head off tooth problems during a longer disruption.
Hand sanitizer
Clean hands before eating and after the bathroom when you can't wash.

👓 Vision

Spare prescription glasses
Critical if you wear them. Lose your only pair and your ability to move, drive, read and spot hazards drops instantly.
Eyeglass cleaner / lens cloth
Keeps glasses, optics and lenses usable through dirt, rain, fingerprints and fog.

🪚 Tools

Knife sharpener
Keeps knives and edged tools sharp — a sharp blade is more useful and safer to use.
Compact saw
Cuts branches and wood far more efficiently than a knife for shelter, firewood and camp work.
Small wrench
Basic mechanical capability for tightening or loosening fittings without a full toolbox.
Cleaning brush
Clears dirt and debris so critical gear keeps working.

🩹 Repair / Improvisation

Heavy-duty tape
Light, quick field repairs for clothing, shelter, straps, containers and gear.
Zip ties
Small, light fasteners for securing loose gear and fast temporary fixes.
Extra strap / webbing
A replacement or improvised support when a strap, buckle, belt or attachment point fails.
Cordage
Shelter lines, repairs, securing loads, hanging gear, replacing broken laces and more.
Bank line or similar utility line
Strong, compact cord for smaller jobs where full-size rope would be overkill.

🥄 Eating & Observation

Titanium spork
A light, tough utensil that does the job of a spoon and a fork.
Binoculars
Look over terrain, routes, obstacles, buildings or activity from a distance before you move closer.

🧴 Equipment Maintenance

Cleaning kit
The supplies to maintain the gear you depend on in the field.
Cleaning snake / bore snake
A compact way to clear fouling or debris from compatible equipment without a bulky cleaning kit.
Lubricating oil
Keeps gear that needs lubrication working. Carry only what you'll realistically use.
Ballistol or similar product
One compact product for cleaning, lubrication and rust protection of compatible gear.

Specialized Gear — Not for Every Bag

These earn their place only if your terrain, your plan and your training call for them. If they don't, leave them out and save the weight.

🍂 Concealment

Camouflage / leaf suit
For situations where staying out of sight is part of your plan. Not a universal requirement.
Camouflage hat / head cover
Pairs with concealment clothing to break up the outline of the head and face.

🧗 Climbing / Rope

Only with real training and terrain that demands it.

Climbing harness
For trained users whose route may require technical climbing or rappelling.
Climbing rope
Technical rope capability where the terrain demands it.
Rappelling carabiner & hardware
Climbing-rated connectors for the specific rope system you're trained to use.

🎯 Ammunition (If Applicable)

Ammunition for your firearm
If a firearm is part of your plan, carry ammunition that matches it (our example kit carries 9mm and 5.56). Ammo is heavy, so balance quantity against total weight.
Hermetically sealed ammo
Protects ammunition from moisture and the elements during long storage or rough travel.

Know and follow the firearm laws of every place your route passes through. See what we stock →

⚠ Gear Is Only as Good as Your Skill With It Practice with everything before you need it: light a fire in the rain, set up your tarp in the dark, filter a bottle of creek water, and walk a few miles with the loaded pack. Wound-care and technical rope gear in particular should only be carried by people trained to use them.
Before You Call It Done

Remember

  • ⚖️
    Only carry what you can transportPack for the distance you realistically expect to travel on foot.
  • 🪶
    Consider lighter alternativesSwap heavy items for lighter ones where it's practical.
  • ✂️
    Avoid unnecessary duplicatesTwo of something critical is smart. Two of everything is a heavy bag.
  • 🔄
    Replace old or heavy itemsUpdate worn gear, check expiration dates and rotate food and medication.
  • 🥾
    Test it by walking with itLoad the bag and walk. You'll learn more in one mile than from any list.
  • 📅
    Know where it isKeep it by a door or in the vehicle, and make sure everyone in the house knows where.

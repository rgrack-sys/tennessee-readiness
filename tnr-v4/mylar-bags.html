Storing Food inMylar Bags

How to pack rice, beans, wheat and other dry staples so they keep for years — not months. Simple, cheap, and you can do it at your kitchen table.

Jump to the Steps Get Mylar Bags Here
The Basics

Why Mylar Works

Stored food is ruined by four things: oxygen, moisture, light and pests. A Mylar bag is a pouch of PET plastic film laminated with a thin layer of metal foil. Sealed shut with an oxygen absorber tucked in, it blocks the first three almost completely — and a bucket around it handles the fourth.

🫧

Stops Oxygen

Oxygen turns oils rancid, fades flavor and lets bugs survive. The absorber pulls it out; the foil keeps it out.

💧

Blocks Moisture

Moisture brings mold and bacteria. Mylar is close to waterproof once heat-sealed.

🌑

Blocks Light

Light breaks down vitamins and color. The foil layer is fully opaque.

📦

Portion Control

Pack in 1-gallon bags and you only open what you'll use, keeping the rest sealed.

Before You Start

What You'll Need

  • 🛍️
    Mylar bagsSuppliers commonly recommend 5 mil or thicker for long-term storage. Quart, 1-gallon and 5-gallon are the common sizes.
  • 🧪
    Oxygen absorbersSmall iron-powder packets, rated in "cc." See the sizing chart below.
  • 🔥
    A heat source to sealAn impulse sealer is easiest. A clothes iron on high (no steam) or a flat hair straightener also works.
  • 🫙
    A mason jarTo hold any unused oxygen absorbers so they don't go to waste.
  • 🖊️
    Permanent markerFor labeling contents and date on every bag.
  • 🪣
    Buckets or totes (recommended)Mylar won't stop a mouse. A lidded bucket or bin protects bags from rodents and punctures.
Step by Step

How to Pack & Seal

Set up your station

Lay out bags, food, a funnel or scoop, your sealer and the marker before you open the oxygen absorbers. Once that packet is opened, the clock starts.

Plug in the iron or sealer now so it's hot and ready.

Fill the bag

Pour in your dry food, leaving 3–4 inches of empty bag at the top so you have room to seal. Tap the bag on the counter to settle the contents and get rid of air pockets.

Wipe the inside of the bag's top edge clean — dust or flour in the seal area causes weak seals.

Add the oxygen absorber

Open the absorber pack, drop the right size absorber on top of the food, and immediately put the leftovers in a mason jar with the lid tight. Absorbers start working the second they meet air, so move quickly — work in batches of 5–10 bags at a time.

Push out the air & seal

Squeeze out as much air as you can, then seal straight across the top. With an iron, lay the bag's top edge over a board or the edge of a table and press firmly for a few seconds. Run a second seal just below the first for insurance.

A good seal looks smooth and even, with no wrinkles or gaps. Tug on it — it should not peel apart.

Label it

Write what's inside and the date packed. If you're storing several sizes, note the weight too.

Check it in a day or two

Within 24–48 hours most bags pull in tight around the food, like it was vacuum-packed. That's the absorber doing its job. Loose, bulky foods like pasta may not look as tight — that's normal. If a bag of rice or flour still looks puffy, cut it open, add a fresh absorber and reseal.

Store it right

Put sealed bags in a bucket or tote and keep them somewhere cool, dark and dry — off the concrete floor. Avoid attics, garages and anywhere that freezes or gets hot.

Sizing Guide

How Big an Oxygen Absorber?

Dense foods like flour and rice leave less air in the bag, so they need less. Loose foods like pasta and beans trap more air and need more.

Bag SizeDense Foods
(rice, flour, wheat)		Loose Foods
(pasta, beans, oats)
Quart100 cc150–300 cc
1 Gallon300–400 cc500 cc
5 Gallon2,000 cc2,500 cc or more

Rule of thumb: these are common supplier guidelines, not lab-tested figures. When in doubt, go bigger — a little extra absorber capacity does no harm, while too little leaves oxygen behind. Check the package your absorbers come in, and ask us if you're not sure. Sugar and salt don't need an absorber.

Choose Wisely

What to Store — and What Not To

Mylar is for dry, low-fat foods. Food should be dry enough to snap, not bend.

Great in Mylar

  • White rice
  • Dried beans, lentils, split peas
  • Wheat berries & other whole grains
  • Rolled oats
  • Dry pasta
  • White flour & cornmeal (shorter life)
  • Nonfat powdered milk
  • Freeze-dried foods
  • Sugar & salt — no absorber needed

Keep Out of Mylar

  • Nuts, seeds & trail mix
  • Whole-wheat flour
  • Granola & anything with added oil or fat
  • Jerky & dried meats
  • Soft or chewy dried fruit
  • Home-dried food that still bends or feels leathery
  • Anything with more than about 10% moisture

A note on brown rice: food-safe oxygen absorbers can be used with it, but its natural oils mean it keeps for a much shorter time than white rice — plan to rotate it within a year or two rather than counting on decades.

⚠ Important: Moisture + No Oxygen = Botulism Risk Oxygen-reducing packaging creates conditions that botulism bacteria can thrive in. Utah State University Extension advises that foods for long-term storage should be about 10% moisture or less, and warns that moist foods sealed this way can cause botulism. If it isn't bone-dry, don't seal it with an absorber.
How Long Will It Last?

Typical Shelf Life

Heat is the big enemy. Citing USDA, Utah State University Extension says shelf life is cut in half for every 10.8°F rise in storage temperature, and recommends a steady 40–60°F.

FoodCommonly Quoted Best-Case Shelf Life
White rice25+ years
Wheat berries25+ years
Dried beans25+ years (older beans take longer to cook)
Rolled oats20+ years
Dry pasta20+ years
Nonfat powdered milk15–20 years
White flour5–10 years
Sugar & saltIndefinitely

These are widely repeated industry estimates for ideal conditions — not guarantees, and not figures from the sources listed below. Real-world results depend on moisture, temperature and how well the bag was sealed. Always check food when you open it — if it smells off, looks wrong or shows signs of moisture or pests, throw it out.

🌡️ Keep it coolA steady 40–60°F is ideal. A basement closet beats a hot garage every time.
🔄 RotateEat from your storage and replace it. First in, first out.
🐭 Guard against crittersRodents chew right through Mylar. Use buckets or bins.
🔁 Reseal after openingCut just below the seal so the bag can be resealed (use a fresh absorber).
